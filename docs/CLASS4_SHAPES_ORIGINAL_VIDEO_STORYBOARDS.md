## Suggested teacher-led video treatments
These are ORIGINAL video-production plans; they do not claim completed media.

### 01 — Meet the shapes you can hold
Opening shot: a reused shoebox, cardboard cube, ball and clean tin on a table (no brand logos).
Beat A: compare flat paper drawing with an object occupying space.
Beat B: teacher points out flat faces and curved surfaces.
Beat C: original digital cube/cylinder/sphere illustration is progressively labelled.
Beat D: student task: locate one sphere and one cuboid nearby.
Mistake to clarify: drawing of a circle versus a solid sphere.
End prompt: "Which of these objects can roll smoothly, and why?"
Safety: no sharp tins or unsafe handling for children.

### 02 — Faces, edges and corners
Opening: teacher holds a cardboard cuboid, colours one flat face, traces an edge and touches a vertex.
Beat A: distinguish flat face from edge boundary.
Beat B: slowly rotate the cube drawing; mark all eight vertices and twelve edges without double counting.
Beat C: count a triangular pyramid's faces/edges/vertices for comparison.
Beat D: student uses a soft or recycled box and labels features.
Mistake: a decorative line on a box is not necessarily a true edge.
End prompt: "Why does a cube have eight corners?"

### 03 — Prisms, pyramids and patterns
Opening: two physical model silhouettes; one with two matching ends, one with triangular sides meeting at apex.
Beat A: compare triangular prism with square pyramid.
Beat B: identify bases and apex in original SVG.
Beat C: count square pyramid (5 faces, 8 edges, 5 vertices).
Beat D: optional extension: F + V − E = 2 for cube and pyramids with flat polygon faces.
End prompt: "How can you distinguish a prism from a pyramid without naming its colour?"

### 04 — From flat paper to a solid
Opening: a recycled cereal box flattened (no copyrighted packaging visible).
Beat A: six connected squares form a net; show an original flat cross.
Beat B: animate conceptual folding of four squares upward.
Beat C: fold final face shut; point out overlap and gaps.
Beat D: parent-supported safe paper folding with no scissors for youngest children.
Mistake: a random six-square layout is not always a foldable cube net.
End prompt: "Why does the arrangement of squares matter?"

### 05 — Angles and strong triangles
Opening: a door opening and square corner of a notebook.
Beat A: compare right/acute/obtuse angles by opening, not arm length.
Beat B: animate a ray rotating to show 45°, 90°, 120°.
Beat C: compare loose four-stick frame with rigid three-stick frame, using safe desk models.
Beat D: student points to one acute and one obtuse opening at home.
Mistake: longer drawn arms do not make an angle larger.
End prompt: "Why is a triangle useful for a frame?"

### 06 — Inside a circle: radius and diameter
Opening: paper circle with visibly marked centre.
Beat A: draw centre-to-edge radius; highlight all equal radii.
Beat B: draw straight line through centre from edge to edge.
Beat C: mark two radii, then demonstrate d=2r using a simple 4 cm → 8 cm model.
Beat D: independent practice on a drawn circle; demonstrate diameter 14 cm → radius 7 cm.
Mistake: a chord that misses the centre is not a diameter.
End prompt: "What makes a diameter different from any other line across a circle?"

## Proposed production requirements
- Teacher recording: natural human explanation, age-appropriate, clearly audible English. Optional voiceover in Kannada can come later for language coverage; keep UI English-first.
- Video: original board drawing, clear labels, 16:9 1080p export, high contrast and captions. Produce a short, intentional explanation per concept; do not pad for viewing time.
- Transcript and subtitles: independently checked for correct vocabulary/pronunciation ("vertex", "vertices", "diameter", "cuboid").
- Accessibility: every key diagram has a textual explanation; no concept relies solely on sound or colour.
- Rights: obtain documented rights for any third-party clip, photo, music or sound. No schoolchildren filmed without appropriate consent.
- Academic sign-off: reviewer tests each of 24 items, checks every number/count and every diagram. Admin checks publisher edition and original-media rights separately.
- Publication: videos and lesson data remain draft until backend editorial workflow moves the unit to published.
- Privacy: no student faces, names, Aadhaar or report cards embedded in public lesson media.

## Minimum quality test before pilot
1. Teacher reads the lesson aloud and judges whether a Class 4 learner understands without prior tuition.
2. A second reviewer checks each question's single correct option and rationale.
3. Test students describe one shape in their own words and answer an unseen follow-up question.
4. Guardian can locate grade, book, chapter, lesson progress and the "why" behind mistakes.
5. Low-end phone can display all labels at 360px without cropped diagrams.
6. Scores remain practice evidence; only staff can confirm official academic mastery separately.
7. Any factual error blocks publication immediately; admin withdrawal must record a reason.
